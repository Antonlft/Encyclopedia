The story: Whirlpool vs. the world

A real case with real receipts. Then we pull out the idea.

  1. 2017
    Whirlpool, America's big washer maker, complains: Samsung and LG are flooding the US with cheap machines.
  2. Jan 2018
    US imposes a safeguard tariff (= emergency tax to shield an industry): 20% on imported washers, 50% above a quota.
  3. 2018
    Washer prices jump +12%. Dryers, which weren't taxed, rise too: +$92. Sellers just tagged along.
  4. The bill
    Economists from the Fed and UChicago add it up:
Extra paid by US shoppers, per year$1.5bn Tariff money collected by the Treasury$82M New US jobs (incl. Samsung's South Carolina plant)~1,800 Cost to consumers per job created~$815,000

Flaaen, Hortaçsu & Tintelnot (2019). Contested: Whirlpool argued the true cost was ~$15–22k per job.

The concept: a tariff raises the price of imports. Local makers raise theirs too, because they can. Shoppers pay; a few producers and workers gain; the government collects a slice.

Remember these 3

It's a tax on your own buyers.Customs bills the importer. In 2025, ~90% of the cost landed on US firms and households (NY Fed).
Protection has a price tag.A few producers gain a lot; millions of shoppers each lose a little. Washers: ~$815k of consumer cost per job.
Every tariff has a second act.Partners retaliate, production reroutes. Soy orders went to Brazil; iPhones to India.
