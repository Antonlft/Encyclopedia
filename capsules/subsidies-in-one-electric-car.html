The story: the $7,500 EV credit

Born in 2008, supercharged in 2022, killed in 2025. A clean natural experiment.

  1. 2022
    The Inflation Reduction Act revamps the credit: up to $7,500, but only for cars and batteries made in North America or allied countries, under price and income caps.
  2. 2023–25
    Loophole: leased EVs dodge the rules through a separate business credit. The leasing company, often the carmaker's own finance arm, claims it and decides how much to pass on.
  3. July 2025
    Congress's One Big Beautiful Bill Act kills the credit. Deadline: 30 Sept 2025.
  4. Q3 2025
    Rush. Record EV sales, a 10.5% market share. GM's EV sales more than double.
  5. Oct 2025 onward
    Cliff. EV share drops from over 11% in September to 5.8% in October. Hyundai replaces the credit with its own $7,500 discount; Honda scraps its 0 Series EVs (March 2026).

The concept: a subsidy drives a wedge between what buyers pay and what sellers get. Both sides share the money, and a big chunk pays people for things they'd have done anyway.
